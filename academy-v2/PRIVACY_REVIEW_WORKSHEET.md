# EFBI privacy and safeguarding review worksheet

Status: working package for Phase 21; not legal advice or launch approval.

This worksheet turns the pre-production checklist into concrete work. “Owner” means Tamerat Gebeyehu or another person he formally assigns. “Reviewer” means a real Ethiopian privacy/legal or child-safeguarding professional, as appropriate.

- who acts as data-protection contact and who covers absences.

## 2. Privacy, safeguarding, and escalation contacts

Owner must still name:

- one safeguarding contact;
- one backup contact for urgent cases and absences;
- an internal response target and safe escalation method;

Technical work: publish only approved contact details; do not expose personal phone numbers by default.

Evidence to keep: contact list, access owners, backup coverage, and a quarterly verification date.

## 3. Lawful basis for every use of data

Technical/privacy work will produce a processing inventory covering account creation, progress, projects, reviews, certificates, security logs, support, deletion, and guardian authorization.

Owner explains why each operation is necessary. The reviewer selects and documents the lawful basis for each purpose. Consent must be separate, informed, specific, clear, active, and withdrawable where it is the selected basis; it must not be bundled with unnecessary processing.

Evidence to keep: one approved processing inventory mapping data, purpose, person, recipient, retention, and lawful basis.

## 4. Learners under 16

Already supplied:

- EFBI intends to serve learners under 16 and learners aged 16 or older.

Owner must still decide:

- the minimum practical enrollment age;
- who may act as parent, guardian, or tutor;
- how EFBI will make reasonable checks without collecting excessive identity documents;
- how authorization is withdrawn and what happens to the learner account;
- when a safeguarding concern overrides routine communications.

Technical work: ordinary under-16 self-registration stays blocked; use an age band instead of full birth date unless review requires more; store versioned authorization evidence; prohibit marketing, profiling, and profile merging for minors.

Reviewer must approve both the legal authorization process and the child-friendly wording before an under-16 account is created.

## 5. Retention periods

Technical work already provides `efbi-retention-v1`, operational reminders, audited holds, controlled deletion, and separate Authentication-removal confirmation.

Owner explains the real operational need for each period. Reviewer accepts or changes every period, especially project/review records, deletion evidence, security logs, and certificate evidence.

Evidence to keep: approved schedule, version, effective date, owner, review date, exceptions, and system test showing deletion works.

## 6. Certificate verification exception

Owner decides whether a certificate should remain publicly verifiable after account deletion and for how long. The learner must separately choose the public certificate name. For an under-16 learner, the reviewed guardian path must cover that public display.

Reviewer must approve the purpose, lawful basis, minimum evidence, public fields, retention period, revocation/replacement behavior, and deletion exception.

Evidence to keep: approved certificate policy, consent wording, sample verification page, and lifecycle test results.

## 7. Legal, safety, fraud, and integrity holds

Owner names the people allowed to request, approve, review, and release a hold. Each reason must be narrow and recorded privately; a hold cannot be used simply because work is delayed.

Reviewer approves permitted reasons, maximum/review periods, access, challenge path, and documentation. Technical work enforces administrator-only access and immutable audit history.

Evidence to keep: hold procedure, role list, 30-day review evidence, and sample audited exercise with synthetic data.

## 8. Google/Firebase, YouTube, email, and international transfers

Technical/privacy work inventories every provider, the information it receives, its purpose, configuration, storage region where known, and relevant contract/privacy terms.

Owner decides whether each provider is necessary and approves any paid plan later. Reviewer assesses processor/recipient roles, contracts, destination countries, safeguards, and whether authority consultation or authorization is required.

Evidence to keep: provider register, applicable terms, configuration screenshots without secrets, transfer assessment, review date, and exit/deletion plan.

## 9. Learner rights and complaints

Technical work designs authenticated or carefully identity-checked routes for access, correction, deletion, restriction, objection, portability where applicable, consent withdrawal, refusal, and complaints. The current deletion workflow is only one part.

Owner names the person who receives and tracks requests and the backup. Reviewer approves identity checks, response wording, timing, exceptions, records, and escalation to the competent authority.

Evidence to keep: rights procedure, request log containing minimum data, response templates, exception approvals, and synthetic exercises.

## 10. Breach and safeguarding response

Owner must name an always-available incident lead and backup and define how team members report a suspected breach immediately.

Technical work prepares containment, evidence preservation, credential rotation, provider escalation, recovery, and communication checklists. Reviewer confirms the legal notification decision process and safeguarding professional confirms child-safety escalation.

The official proclamation contains 72-hour notification and communication provisions. Treat every suspected breach urgently; a qualified reviewer must interpret the exact application and exceptions.

Evidence to keep: incident plan, contact tree, blank incident record, notification templates, and a dated tabletop exercise.

## 11. Data protection impact assessment

Technical/privacy work prepares the first draft from the real architecture and tests. It must describe processing and purposes, assess necessity and proportionality, identify risks to people, and record safeguards and remaining risk.

Owner verifies that the description matches actual EFBI operations and accepts or rejects each proposed mitigation. Privacy and safeguarding reviewers challenge the assessment, decide whether residual risk is acceptable, and determine whether prior consultation or authorization is required.

Evidence to keep: versioned DPIA, risk decisions, reviewer comments, owner acceptance, review date, and a trigger list requiring reassessment after material changes.
