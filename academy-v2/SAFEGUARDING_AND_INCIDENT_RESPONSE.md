## When a concern arrives

Treat a concern as urgent when someone may be in immediate danger. EFBI email is not an emergency service. A trusted adult or appropriate local emergency service should be contacted, followed by the designated safeguarding lead.

For every child-safety concern:

1. Stay calm and take the report seriously.
2. Focus first on the learner's immediate safety and wellbeing.
3. Do not investigate, decide whether abuse occurred, or ask probing or leading questions.
4. Explain what will happen next. Do not promise secrecy when information may need to be shared to keep someone safe.
5. Share information only with people who need it to protect the learner and handle the concern.
6. Record the concern consistently and keep it confidential.

## Data or account incident

Examples include an exposed learner record, compromised operator account, unexpected public access, unsafe content, lost administrator device, or suspicious certificate operation.

1. **Identify:** record when the issue was noticed, the affected system, and the smallest useful factual description.
2. **Contain:** close enrollment or the affected workflow, sign out compromised sessions, remove unnecessary operator access, and preserve evidence.
3. **Protect:** stop further learner-data exposure and keep communication private.
4. **Assess:** identify the learner records, people, systems, and time period involved without opening unrelated data.
5. **Escalate:** involve the safeguarding lead, backup, and appropriate technical or external adviser.
6. **Communicate:** notify affected people or authorities only through the final reviewed procedure. Do not invent a legal deadline.
7. **Recover:** restore only verified-safe services, rotate compromised access where necessary, and confirm controls work.
8. **Review:** record the cause, corrective actions, owner, completion date, and prevention change.

## Minimum incident record

Keep only:

- a unique incident identifier;
- detection date and reporting route;
- affected system and learner or operator identifier when necessary;
- short factual description;
- priority and immediate-safety assessment;
- containment actions and times;
- assigned handler and escalation decisions;
- necessary communications;
- recovery verification;
- closure date and prevention action.

Never place passwords, verification codes, full identity documents, unrelated health details, or speculative accusations in the incident record. Private safeguarding details must never appear in learner-visible review feedback.

1. Name the safeguarding lead and trusted backup.
2. Confirm both people understand the role and have a private, reliable contact route.
3. Build a reviewed local directory for emergency, child-protection, legal, and specialist referrals.
4. Decide where confidential incident records will be stored and who can access them.
5. Review notification duties and deadlines under Ethiopian law with a qualified reviewer.
6. Rehearse one child-safety scenario and one account/data incident.
7. Record gaps, update this playbook, and complete the final DPIA review.
8. Keep ages 12–15 closed until their entire guardian and safeguarding route is approved.

## Authoritative references used

- [Ethiopian Ministry of Justice — Personal Data Protection Proclamation No. 1321/2024](https://justice.gov.et/en/law/personal-data-protection-proclamation/)
- [UNICEF Child Rights and Business — Child Safeguarding Toolkit](https://www.unicef.org/childrightsandbusiness/media/1681/file/Child-Safeguarding-Toolkit-2026.pdf)

The UNICEF toolkit supports a child-centered response, a named safeguarding lead and deputy, clear reporting, minimal confidential records, immediate protection, and referral to appropriate trained or local authorities. EFBI still needs Ethiopia-specific review before relying on this playbook for launch.
